# Judging the qBraid Award

Two qBraid judges score each showcase out of 100, separately from track judging. A team's track result doesn't affect this award.

## Rubric

Score each criterion anywhere from 0 to its maximum. The columns describe a full score, about half, and close to zero.

| Criterion | Points | Full marks | About half | Close to zero |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Creativity** | 30 | qBraid is used in a way we didn't expect, or on a part of the problem other teams didn't touch | A sensible use we've seen before, like running the starter circuit on hardware | A tutorial or demo run unchanged |
| **Central to the project** | 25 | The result depends on qBraid: hardware or GPU runs drive the team's conclusion | qBraid ran part of the work, but the conclusions come mostly from elsewhere | qBraid was only where the notebook happened to run |
| **Verified** | 20 | Job IDs or links check out, simulator and hardware results are compared, noise is explained, and the cost is shown | Some evidence, but incomplete or hard to match to the claims | No evidence, or it doesn't match the claims |
| **Visualization and story** | 15 | One clear visual makes the qBraid result obvious to someone outside quantum | Charts are there but need explaining | Raw output or walls of text |
| **Reproducible** | 10 | A judge can relaunch it in one click (Launch on qBraid button or shared environment) | The code is shared but setup is unclear | Nothing to rerun |

## Rules judges hold to

- **Spending doesn't score.** More credits or more jobs earn nothing by themselves. Careful, cheap use, such as batching circuits or choosing shot counts on purpose, counts toward Verified.
- **Depth beats breadth.** One qBraid feature used well beats five used briefly.
- **Track judging is separate.** Track judges can skip the qBraid Award slides, and a strong or weak track result doesn't move this score.
- **Conflicts of interest:** if a judge wrote code for a team during the event, that judge doesn't score that team, and the other judge's score stands alone.

## Process

1. After submissions close, each judge scores every entry independently.
2. Average the two judges' scores for each team. If they're more than 15 points apart, the judges talk it through first, and either can change their score.
3. Check the job IDs or launch links for the top three, and talk to each of those teams for two minutes if the schedule allows.
4. The highest average wins. The winner is announced at the closing ceremony.

## Tie-breakers

1. Higher Creativity score
2. Higher Verified score
